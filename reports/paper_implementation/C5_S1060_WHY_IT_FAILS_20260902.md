# C5 seed 1060 — exactly why it fails

Date: 2026-09-02. Diagnosis only; no behaviour changed.
Source: `reports/paper_implementation/c5_s1060_coverage_prompt_v2_analysis.json`
(59 decisions, exact replay, 0.0 m reproduction error).

## The failure is not grounding, and not the prompt

| | |
|---|---|
| decision frames | 59 |
| frames in which the target is **visible** | **0** |
| frames in which the goal is geometrically **inside the 90° FOV** | **23 (39%)** |
| median absolute goal bearing | **73°** |
| commanded bearings actually used | −32° … +35° (of ±45° available) |
| decisions where the goal was inside the FOV and the VLM steered >15° away | **14** |
| depth directly ahead, median | 7.0 m; under 3 m on 22% of decisions |

The target is never visible even though it is in front of the camera on two
decisions in five. The difference between "in the field of view" and "visible"
is **occlusion**: the goal is behind obstacles.

So the episode fails for two distinct reasons that need different fixes.

## Failure A — 61% of the time the drone is not facing the goal

The decision agent is sent exactly one image, the current frame
(`onfly.py`, `images=(_encode_png(image),)`), and answers with a pixel `(u,v)`
inside it. **The set of headings it can express is therefore ±45°, the camera's
half-angle.** With a median goal bearing of 73°, the correct direction is
literally not in the answer space on most decisions.

This is why the prompt-only viewpoint experiment
(`C5_VIEWPOINT_PROMPT_EXPERIMENT_20260902.md`) was rejected at 0/89 and 0/89:
no wording can make a model select a direction it cannot name. The history and
latest-emphasis sheets do not change this — they are used by the *monitor*, not
by the decision agent, and in any case they show frames from where the vehicle
has already been rather than what lies off its current axis.

`frontier_assist` does not close the gap either. When the target is not visible
it hops further along the **initial heading**, which is a straight line, not
coverage, and cannot turn toward a goal at −71°.

## Failure B — 39% of the time it is facing the goal and still cannot see it

On those 23 decisions the target is inside the frustum and occluded. The VLM is
behaving correctly: there is nothing to point at. No widening of the answer
space fixes this, because the information is not in any image available from
where the vehicle is standing.

This is the same occlusion result found earlier in the project on the scripted
policies, and it is the half that matches the intuition that **the value lies
past the obstacles rather than where the vehicle can already see**. Acting on it
requires choosing a viewpoint for what it would *reveal*, which needs a
representation of what has and has not been observed. The VLM has neither.

## What follows

* Refining the decision prompt cannot fix either failure. A cannot be expressed;
  B is not present in the pixels. This is a mechanism gap, and the prompt
  experiment already spent two seeds establishing it.
* The cheapest decisive test of the "one rotation would have told it" intuition
  is to widen the answer space to 360° — give the decision agent a panorama at
  the current position and let `(u,v)` index that instead of a single frame.
  That addresses **A only**, and should be judged on whether target-visible
  frames rise above zero, not on success.
* **B needs unobserved-volume reasoning**, not a bigger picture. That is a real
  addition to OnFly's mechanism and has to be declared as such rather than
  slipped in, since OnFly does not have it.

Nothing here is fixed by colour detection, simulator truth, scripted target
search or a weaker substrate, and none of those may be used to close it.
